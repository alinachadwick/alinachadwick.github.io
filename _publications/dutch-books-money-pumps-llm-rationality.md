LLMs commonly fail to be probabilistically coherent and to have transitive preferences, making them vulnerable to Dutch books and money pumps as they are increasingly used as agents in real-world tasks. We frame this as an aggregation problem and introduce a dedicated rationality layer that uses methods from social epistemology and social choice theory, including a novel hierarchical voting rule, to make LLM outputs coherent and transitive in an explainable way.
